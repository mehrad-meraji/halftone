The dot screen is Halftone's one comic nod. It is a **stage texture, not a surface**: it marks places that frame or show something, and it fades out. It never sits behind text you have to read.

## Where it goes

- **Header strips** of containers: card, dialog, alert dialog and sheet. Use `--ht-halftone`.
- **Stages**: a surface that exists to show or frame something, such as a hero, an example area, an empty state, a sign-in side panel or a spotlight. Use `--ht-halftone-stage`, and fade it with a mask.

## Where it never goes

- Behind body copy, inputs, tables, lists or code.
- On menus, select lists, popovers, tooltips or toasts. Those are glass or solid.
- On error states. An error uses the alert icon, not decoration.

## Spotlight

A stage can follow the pointer: a faint accent glow and a denser dot screen appear under it and fade when it leaves. The docs stages use [`ht-spotlight`](../../components/spotlight/), and [`ht-empty-state`](../../components/empty-state/) has the same effect built in (turn it off with `no-spotlight`).

- It is decoration only: it ignores touch and keyboard, it is off under reduced motion, and it does not take pointer events from the controls under it.
- On a stage it sits behind the content. It is not used on an error state.

## Tokens

| Token | Tile | Dot | Dark mode | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--ht-halftone` | 8px | fades out by 2.3px, accent at 20% | accent at 28% | Header strips |
| `--ht-halftone-stage` | 10px | fades out by 2.8px, accent at 13% | accent at 20% | Large stages |

Each dot fades from its centre to nothing, so the dots look soft and slightly blurred. They are far apart, so the screen reads as a texture, not a grid. Dark mode takes a little more accent, because purple dots fade on a dark ground.

Both become `none` under `forced-colors: active` and `prefers-contrast: more`. To remove the dots everywhere, set them to `none`:

```css
:root {
  --ht-halftone: none;
  --ht-halftone-stage: none;
}
```

Text on a patterned area still has to keep 4.5 to 1 contrast against the brightest point of the dots.
